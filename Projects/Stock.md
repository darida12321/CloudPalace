Stock is often made from [[Water|water]] and [[Meat|meat]] scraps and vegetables. 
- **Connective tissues** for **collagen**, which turns into **gelatin** when heated.
- **Muscles** for flavour. Chop the meat small for faster flavour transfer.

**Collagen** is like a twisted up yarn, which takes about 4h on a simmer to untangle regardless of temperature. It's a chemical process, so unlike flavour, this can't be sped up. Any longer and the strands start to break down.

Store-bought **gelatin** can help though. With chopped [[Chicken|chicken]], about 45mins is enough to extract all flavour. 

Traditional French stock aims to be clear, so it simmers the [[Water|water]], then removes the fat and gunk from the top. We can go for a hard boil to disperse the fat, making the stock more foggy, but more flavourful.

Still, a layer of fat on top is bad. You can let the stock sit for 15 mins, then ladle it. Or, wait overnight while it's in the fridge, so the fat becomes a solid.

Stock keeps for 5 days, or can be frozen as ice cubes, or freezer bags for 3 months.
