# The embodiment of basic arithmetic


##### Number discrimination by babies



[[Infants discriminating between numbers]]

After 3-4 days, infants can discriminate between collections of 2 and 3 items:

Antell & Keating, 1983 https://psycnet.apa.org/record/1983-27708-001 took 65 infants under 7 days old, 25 fell asleep during the experiment, and thus were discarded as data points.

They were shown white cards with some number of black dots.

Why only now?


We needed techniques to check what's going on in the minds of infants.
- Friedman 1972 https://psycnet.apa.org/record/1972-31202-001, it was measured using [[Habituation]] that infants pay more attention to novel patterns.
Babies have some elementary pattern recognition
- Fantz 1963 https://psycnet.apa.org/record/1964-02380-001, infants under 5 days old look at complex textures for longer, therefore can percieve form.
- Fantz 1965 https://psycnet.apa.org/record/1966-13062-001, infants actually selectively pay attention to patterns of objects and surfaces.

The notion that we should pay attention to how infants learn to perceive.
- Gibson 1969 https://psycnet.apa.org/record/1969-35014-000 defines the domain of **perceptual learning**. Infants don't learn by association, but by differentiation (WIKIPEDIA)
It was taught that counting would only come later.
- J. Mehler and T. G. Bever 1967 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6054816/, **Cognitive capacity of young children**: Children between 2y6m and 3y2m can discriminate between the relative number of objects, between 3y2m and 4y6m they pick the longer row as opposed to the more objects. after 4y6m, they can discriminate correctly.
- Starkey & Cooper 1980 https://psycnet.apa.org/record/1981-27833-001, 4 month old babies can **subitize**.
It was believed that babies do not have the necessary eye sight for this test.
- Dayton, Jones & Aui 1964 https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4684-2187-3_6, the [[Visual acuity]] of infants below 1mo is 20/150 and 20/400. Their hearing, however, is similar to an adult.
