Butter consists of a few different parts, that can be easily be separated out by heating:
- **Butterfat**: 80% (84% for high-end, 65% for fresh-churned). It has a large variety of proteins that cause changes in the texture when heated. Liquifies at around 35C
- **Water**: 15% (11% for high-end, 30% for fresh-churned). This is what causes bubbles and foams when heated. When the butter no longer foams, the heating is done.
- **Milk proteins**: 5%. It's the white scum that float when heated. These are what burn when butter is overheated.

Regular butter is not actually ideal for searing food. Raw **butterfat** (**clarified butter** or **ghee**) is a lot better.
