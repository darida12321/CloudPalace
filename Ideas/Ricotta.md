**Ricotta** (meaning recooked) is made by adding acid to heated [[Whey|whey]] (typically from [[Pecorino Romano]]). The heat and acid causes the proteins (**casein**) to bind, trapping moisture and fat, and making soft white curds. Then you carefully remove the curds, and decrease the water content.

Most stores skip the water draining step, and add gums and stabilizers to keep the water. As such, you should make your own [[Fresh ricotta|fresh ricotta]] if possible.

The biggest influence on the flavour is what acid you use:
- [[Buttermilk]]: Very sour, very curdy.
- [[Vinegar]]: Carefully controlled acid, easy to use.
- [[Lemon|Lemon juice]]: Adds a small sourness. The PH of lemons varies a bit, so it's inconsistent.

Next up is how much time you spend draining. (drain with a cheese cloth over a strainer).
- **Under 5 mins**: Moist and creamy. Great with [[Olive oil|olive oil]] or [[Honey|honey]].
- **15-20 mins**: Spreadable. Good for savoury applications like lasagne or pizza.
- **Over 2h**: Dry crumbly malleable curds. Good for cakes and pasta.

The pasteurization of the milk also has some effect. **UHT** [[Milk|milk]] curdles less as its long proteins have broken down.
