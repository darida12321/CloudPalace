An **atom** is the smallest unit of chemical elements. It consists of a [[Nucleus|nucleus]] of [[Proton|protons]] and [[Neutron|neutrons]], and an [[Electron|electron]] cloud around it.

The number of [[Proton|protons]] and [[Electron|electrons]] cancel out, resulting in an outwardly neutral [[Electromagnetism|electric charge]]. The most important property for the classification of an atom is the number of [[Proton|protons]] (and therefore [[Electron|electrons]]) it has. This is called their [[Atomic number|atomic number]].

**Atoms** can appear with a different number of [[Neutron|neutrons]]. These are called [[Isotopes|isotopes]]. There are about 339 naturally occurring [[Nucleus|nuclides]], of which 251 are not [[Radioactivity|radioactive]]. These are the [[Isotopes|stable isotopes]]. 

The [[Electron|electrons]] around it form shells. The number of atoms on the outer shell has a large influence on its chemical reactivity. As such, the chemical properties of atoms are periodically repeating. This results in the [[Periodic table|periodic table]].
