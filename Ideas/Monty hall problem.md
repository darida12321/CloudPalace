Suppose you're on a game show and presented with three doors: two has nothing behind them, one has a car. You have to pick a door. Then the host opens up a door that has nothing behind it, and gives you the option to switch doors. Should you?

The intuitive answer is that it shouldn't matter. There are two doors left: one with nothing behind it and one with a car behind it. However, switching doors actually gives you better odds.

You can see this by applying the [[Total probability theorem|total probability theorem]] you can solve it.
$$
\begin{align}
P(\text{other door has car})&= P(\text{you picked car})*0 + P(\text{you picked empty})*1\\
&=1/3*0+2/3*1\\
&=2/3
\end{align}
$$
