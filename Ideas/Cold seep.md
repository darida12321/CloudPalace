These form when oceanic plates are subducted. Organic compounds degrade over time and produce methane. Tectonic compression forces methane up, where anaerobic microbes under the sediment oxidize it using sulphides, producing hydrogen sulfide. This is consumed by other aerobic [[Chemosynthesis|chemosynthetic]] microbes on the sediment.

The organisms here capture a large amount of methane, stopping it from reaching the atmosphere. This is called the [[Benthic filter]].

##### Life
Unlike most of the abyssal plain, these **cold seeps** are booming with life. Many denizens are similar to the ones found in [[Hydrothermal vent|hydrothermal vents]].
- **Deep-sea mussel**: Covers whole fields. They host the bacteria in their gills.
- **Tube worms**: The ground is soft, they burrow down for sulphites, forming bushes.
- **Yeti crabs**: Farm the bacteria on hair-like filaments.

There are predators with higher [[Trophic level|trophic levels]] too. Octopuses, giant crabs, other [[Grazer|grazers]] and scavengers.

##### Varieties
There are many varieties of seeps, each hosting different species.
- **Mud volcanoes**: Methane rises from over 1km deep, surfaces in pulses, liquifies mud.
- **Hydrate seeps**: In high-pressures and low-temperatures, yellow frozen sulphides exist.
- **Asphalt seeps**: Petroleum leaked out. Resembles a cooled lava field.
	- Little is known about these. So called "tar lilies" have been found.
- **Brine pools**: Deeper, more buoyant salt layer pushes to the surface of overlying sediment.
	- Sometimes forms a denser, 5x more salty, methane containing brine pool.

Cold seeps don't last forever. The process creating hydrogen sulphite from methane also releases bicarbonate ions. These react with calcium in rocks, creating carbonate reefs and tubes. But these eventually block the flow of gasses.

This process might take over 15,000 years. However, afterwards, it might provide solid anchor-points amidst seas of soft mud, used by coral reefs.
