Bees are able to [[Tool use|use tools]]. **Olli Loukola** performed a set of experiments that showed this. At first, he would teach bees to pull strings to get rewards. Then he switched to more complex tasks.

He showed a bee that rolling a ball to a marked area would result in the release of some sugar. The bee learned this behaviour, and started rolling the ball by itself.

##### How learning occurs
To figure out how bees learn, he created a few groups of observer bees who would get sugar water when the ball reaches the centre:
- Group 1 would see another bee roll the ball in the marked area. 99% success rate.
- Group 2 would see a "ghost" (magnet) roll the ball to the goal. 78% success rate.
- Group 3 would see no demonstration, just the ball already in the goal. 34% success rate.

Even more impressive, during the presentation, only the furthest ball was moved. The rest were glued down. But the observer bees usually went for the closest ball.

##### Abstract reasoning
Further tests were conducted to see if bees are capable of advanced reasoning.

First, they were given a similar task, but only a ball the same colour as the platform would result in a reward. They "graduated" when they did it correctly 5 consecutive times.

Then a new platform colour they haven't seen before was introduced, with interesting results:
- If the new platform was yellow, they passed 86% of the time.
- If the new platform was red, they passed 43% of the time.
- If the new platform was blue, they passed 0% of the time (maybe fear of new things).

##### Complex tool usage
A variation of the [[Box and banana experiment|box and banana experiment]] was also conducted. Bees were shown a "flower" (blue dot) with sugar water on top. They had to roll a ball out of a corridor to get to it.

After the bee learned that the ball is safe, and the "flower" contained sugar water, they put the flower on the top of the room. It was only 3cm tall, so the bee couldn't fly to it (it's too clumsy). They had to roll the ball in place and climb it. They succeeded.

Bees do have a tendency to prefer playing with balls over getting food though, so maybe this was an accident. A new experiment was conducted where there was a wall with a door between the ball and the "flower", so the bees would have to make the conscious choice to roll the ball to the other side. 75% succeeded.

Even with different room setups or mazes, the bees succeeded.
