Discovered in **1977** by the **Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution** near **Galapagos**. There were geologists, geochemists and geophysicists on board, but no biologists... nobody expected to find vents teeming with life.

Hydrothermal vents form at earth's plate boundaries where they drift apart and magma is closer to the surface. Water seeps into the rocks, gets superheated, dissolves minerals and billows out in 400C smoke plumes, forming chimneys.
- **Black smokers**: High in Iron Sulphide, hottest. 
- **White smoker**: Barium, Calcium, Silicon.
- **Other**: Shimmering streams of water

##### Life
The sulfides from the vents are broken down by [[Chemosynthesis|chemosynthetic]] [[Autotroph|autotrophs]]. Then other organisms ([[Heterotroph|heterotrophs]]) consume those microbes.
- **Squat lobsters**: feed on the microbial mats surrounding the chimney.
- **Deep sea mussels**: Feed on microbes suspended in the water.
- **Yeti crabs**: Farm the bacteria on hair-like filaments.
- **Pompei worm**: Farm the bacteria similar to yeti crab, but has a higher heat tolerance.
- **Tube worms**: Sessile, but hold some microbes in their tubes in endosymbiosis.
- **Rimicaris exoculata shrimp**: The *exoculata* means "no eyes", but it was later found that they do have something similar! It detects the black-body radiation of the plumes. We don't know how they use this information.

There are predators with higher [[Trophic level|trophic levels]] too:
- **Hydrothermal vent eelpout**: Consume tube worms.
- **Deep-sea octopus**: Apex predator. 
- **Pacific white skate**: Comes here to lay eggs for a faster hatching time.

All together, over 800 animal species have been discovered in vent systems. Different geographic areas have different species. In the Mid-Atlantic ridge, shrimps replace tube worms.

For a few hundred meters, abyssal plane fauna has a higher abundance due to the increase in organic matter.

##### Network of vents
Hydrothermal vents mostly occur on tectonic plate boundaries::
- **East Pacific Rise**: Fast-spreading ridge, 10s of kms apart.
- **Mid-Atlantic Ridge**: Slow-spreading ridge, 100s of kms apart.

However, these sites are volcanically active. When we returned to the one found at Galapagos, it was paved over due to an eruption. How do these creatures migrate?

After the eruption and re-establishment of a vent, we found larvae from 350km away. Jet currents along the ridges transports them large distances. These larvae can also survive over 40 days, get picked up by the vent, and settle back to the same area a month later.

Larvae are too small to tag and too few in number to sample. Most of our knowledge comes from simulations, and the observation of die samples.
