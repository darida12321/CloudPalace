Food is scarce on the sea floor, so even wood-eating organisms have evolved. However, wood is extremely hard to break down. Only some rare bacteria and fungi do it. There are plenty of animals that host and aid these bacteria in endosymbiosis:
- **Wood-boring bivalve**: A living chisel, a species exclusive to wood falls.
- **Giant shipworm**: Use their hard shells to bore through wood.
- **Wood-eating squat lobster**: Claws that tear away at the wood surface.

The big difference between breaking down wood in a forest vs underwater is that the microbes doing it are anaerobic. This creates sulphides, used by [[Chemosynthesis|chemosynthetic]] bacteria, which feed ecosystems similar to those found in [[Hydrothermal vent|hydrothermal vents]] and [[Cold seep|cold seeps]].

Wood takes about 100 years to fully decay. leaving behind the iron components. However, these bacteria can't live in the cold Antarctic seas, so ships like the "Endurance" remain intact.

Iron ships cannot be consumed, but they still provide oases of life. They give shelter to fish. Corals and barnacles can attach to it, unlike the soft mud lands. They also provide elevation, giving access to faster ocean currents.
