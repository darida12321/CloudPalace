The ocean floor contains geologic trenches where tectonic plates subsume. These places offer a different topology, and therefore host a different set of organisms than the [[Abyssal plain|abyssal plain]]. This shift starts at around a depth of 6km.

One of the most well known examples of this is the **Mariana Trench**, whose deepest point, the **Challenger Deep**, sits at a depth of 11.3km.

##### Life
[[Chemosynthesis|Chemosynthetic]] microbial mats cover the bottom. These were found to be **barophilic**, meaning they thrived under high pressure. Later, more complex life was found.

The pressure below 5kms dissolves the calcium-carbonate based bones of vertebrates, and shells of amphipods. Regardless, complex life was found. 
- **Hadal amphipod**: Shield their shells using aluminium coating, gathered from seafloor mud.
- **Holothurians**: Sea cucumbers scavenging the mud.
- **Mariana snailfish**: Can live as deep as 8km. 
- **Xenophyonophores**: Single celled organism with 20cm diameter.
- [[Sea squirt]]: Anchor themselves to the side of canyons, wait for passing prey.
