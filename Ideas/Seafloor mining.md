There is an estimated 600 [[Hydrothermal vent|hydrothermal vents]] at any one time, whose plumes produce underwater sulphide systems containing copper. Many more might have been produced already.

However, this might endanger some of these vent ecosystems. Companies claim that vent species thrive and repopulate after volcanic eruptions, so surely mining won't have a big effect. Biologists retort with the notion that these disturbances can cause unknown side effects, like enabling only some species to spread. "The result could be the subsea equivalent of replacing an old-growth forest with a field of dandelions"

##### Attempts
In 1997 Papua New Guinea gave mining rights to **Nautilus Minerals**, who to grind the copper-rich rock on the seafloor around  vacuum up the slurry and pump it to a ship. (The local villagers were not happy, and asked the marine biologists to help attack Nautilus's ships). However, the price of copper dropped and the plan halted in 2008.

Currently, the **Metals Company** is attempting to pick up the mantle. They reject scientist's worries that deep-see mining has terrible long-term effects on the ecosystem.

##### Law
In 1994, the **International Seabed Authority (ISA)** was created, and now has 171 member nations. Its goals are to control mining operations, and protect the ecosystem of the seabed. 

So far it has approved over two dozen ocean floor mining explorations, but hasn't issued any commercial mining contracts.

Still, it also has plenty of controversies. It has no transparency. Some of its leaders are connected to mining corporations. It was already scheduled to approve mining operations multiple times despite scientist's concerns, and some countries like the USA still hasn't joined.

In fact, **The Metals Company** has created a USA subsidiary (TMC USA) which under the **1980 Deep Seabed Hard Mineral Resources Act (DSHMRA**), applied for a mining permit via the **National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA)**. The ISA explicitly condemned this.

- **April 2025**: Trump issued an executive order, making deep-sea mining a national priority.
- **January 2026**: NOAA updated its application and review process. TMC USA applied.
- **October 2026**: NOAA will hold a virtual hearing.
