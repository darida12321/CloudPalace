Mr. Smith has two children. At least one of them is a boy. What's the probability of the other child being a boy?

The intuitive answer is $1/2$, as one child's gender shouldn't affect the other's. However, this is wrong.

This is a case of [[Conditional probability|conditional probability]]: $P(\text{two boys }|\text{ at least one boy})$.
The [[Sample space|sample space]] has four possible outcomes: $BB, BG, GB, GG$.
$P(\text{at least one boy})=3/4$
$P(\text{two boys})=1/4$

So the conditional probability is $\frac{1/4}{3/4}=1/3$.
